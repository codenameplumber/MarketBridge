1. Who writes the signals

Every signal and every piece of analysis on KonfluenX is written by a platform user. KonfluenX does not author trading calls.

Signal providers are independent individuals. They are not our employees, agents or partners, they are not vetted as financial professionals, and we do not review their reasoning before publication.

Following a provider is your decision, made on evidence we try hard to keep honest. It is not a recommendation from us.

2. Two kinds of signal, and why the difference matters

KonfluenX separates signals into two types at every level of the product. Recognising which one you are looking at is the single most useful thing on this page.

Executable signals

These map to a Deriv contract that KonfluenX can genuinely configure and buy. They show Trade This Signal, which opens a pre-filled trade ticket. Their results are resolved from the actual contract outcome.

Analysis signals

These are trade ideas — entry zone, stop loss, take-profit levels — that do not correspond to a Deriv contract we can execute from here. They show View Market, never a trade button. Their results are resolved against the price feed.

We built this separation because the alternative — a trade button that quietly fails or executes something other than what was described — costs people money. If you see a trade button here, it works.

3. How results are decided

A signal provider does not get to tell us whether they won. Results are resolved from evidence, and every result carries the method used:

  • From the contract — for executable signals, the outcome is read from the real Deriv contract.
  • From the price feed — for analysis signals, the published stop loss and take-profit levels are checked against recorded market prices.
  • Provider claim — used only where neither of the above is possible. Results resolved this way are labelled unverified everywhere they appear, and are visibly distinct.
  • Administrator — used for corrections and disputes, and logged in the audit trail.

4. Why the win rates are hard to fake

The obvious way to manufacture a 90% win rate is to publish a hundred signals and delete the losers. Four mechanisms stop that:

  1. Publishing locks the signal. A tamper-evident snapshot is taken. Entry, direction, levels and parameters cannot be changed afterwards — only clarifying commentary can be appended, and it is shown as an addition.
  2. Deleting is retracting, not erasing. A retracted signal leaves the feed but stays in the provider's published history and stays in the denominator of their win rate.
  3. Results come from evidence. See section 3.
  4. Rankings have a minimum sample. A provider with one winning signal is at 100%, but they do not appear on a leaderboard. Their profile says the sample is too small rather than quietly showing an impressive number.

This makes the numbers meaningfully harder to game. It does not make them a prediction.

5. What a win rate actually tells you

A win rate is a description of the past. Read it carefully:

  • 78% still loses roughly one trade in four. Losing streaks are normal and expected.
  • Win rate says nothing about size. A provider can win 80% of the time and still lose money overall if the losses are larger than the wins. Look at average risk/reward alongside it.
  • Sample size matters more than the number. 70% over 200 signals is far stronger evidence than 90% over 10.
  • Market conditions change. A strategy that performed well in one regime can stop working without warning.
  • Your result will differ. Different entry timing, stake and exit discipline produce a different outcome from the same signal.

6. What "Boosted" means

KonfluenX administrators can boost a signal, which gives it greater visibility in feeds and on market pages for a limited period.

A boost is a visibility decision, not a quality judgement. It does not mean we have verified the analysis, that we agree with it, or that we expect it to win. It carries no guarantee of any kind.

Boosts are time-boxed, labelled on the signal, and recorded in our administrative audit log.

7. Your responsibility

When you act on a signal, the trade is yours. You chose to take it, you chose your stake, and you own the outcome.

  • Do your own analysis before acting on anyone else's.
  • Never risk more than you can afford to lose.
  • Understand the contract type before you trade it.
  • Position size according to your own risk management, not the signal author's.
  • Remember that a signal you copy late is a different trade from the one that was published.

8. Reporting a signal

If you believe a signal is fraudulent, fabricated, misleading, or is being used to solicit funds or promote an off-platform paid service, report it.

Use the report option on the signal itself, or email moderation@vybe.app. Reports are anonymous to the person reported and are reviewed by the moderation team.
